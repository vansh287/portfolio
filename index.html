I teach machines to read medical scans.

I am a fourth-year BS-MS student in applied mathematics at IISER Thiruvananthapuram. My work sits where computer vision meets medicine: segmenting what matters in a scan, turning it into numbers that hold up, and predicting something a clinician actually needs to know.

What I care about most is generalisation. A model that scores well on the hospitals it trained on can fall apart at one it has never seen, and I would rather have a small model whose reasoning I can follow than a large one I have to trust on faith. I like taking an idea from the mathematics underneath it down to a reproducible implementation somebody else can run.

Right now: building the B-mode radiomics pipeline for QuantUS at TU Dresden, and scoping a master's thesis for next year. Open to conversations about either.

03

Publication

MICCAI 2026 · HECKTOR Challenge

Geometry Meets Metabolism: Interpretable Segmentation, Staging, and Prognosis in Head and Neck PET/CT

V. Pandit et al. · team Vision-II · August 2026

Ranked in the top 15 of the challenge

The challenge pools head-and-neck PET/CT scans from several hospitals and asks three clinical questions at once: outline the primary tumour and the involved lymph nodes, assign the tumour and nodal stage, and predict recurrence-free survival. We built one pipeline for all three, and designed every component for stability across centres rather than for the leaderboard.

That constraint decided the design. Nodal stage is read directly off a few geometric measurements of the segmented nodes, chiefly a greatest-dimension diameter that mirrors the size criterion a radiologist already uses, then refined by a fixed rule on metabolic activity. Tumour stage fuses a tabular classifier with a convolutional network on a tumour-centred crop. Survival is a Cox model over clinical variables and predicted stage, with imaging deliberately left out because it is the most scanner-dependent part of the pipeline. Every staging and survival component was selected by its accuracy on a hospital held out during development, not by its average.

Two results I did not expect: for nodal staging the plain geometric head beat every image model we tried, on the average centre and on the weakest one; and for survival, nothing with more capacity beat a linear model.

04

Research

Reading ultrasound before it becomes a picture

I develop the core B-mode radiomics pipeline of QuantUS, an open-source quantitative ultrasound platform. It computes first-order and GLCM texture features directly from raw RF signals, before the beamformed grey image most people picture as an ultrasound exists.

The features are the easy half. The hard half is making a number mean the same thing on two different machines, so I implemented phantom-based normalisation for device-independent, reproducible results. I also work on backend refactoring and GUI integration, because a method is only useful if a clinical researcher can reach it.

Ongoing QuantUS · TU Dresden Jan 2026 →

Segmenting things no dataset warned you about

A vision system that only recognises what it was shown is a liability on a real road. I built a pipeline for semantic segmentation of both known and novel objects, extending AIDE with lightweight captioning models (GLAMM, CODA-LM) so the system can describe what it cannot yet classify. Prototyped on Cityscapes, benchmarked on IDD, IDD-Anomaly and Carla-Anomaly.

This is the project that decided the rest. Segmentation was the first time I could watch the mathematics do something visible.

IISER Thiruvananthapuram Aug – Dec 2025

Rebuilding machine learning before trusting it

A summer implementing classical and Bayesian machine-learning models from scratch in NumPy, then comparing them honestly against their scikit-learn equivalents on real data. The library call is one line; knowing why it converges is the education.

Other work

Teaching a drone to stay out of sight

A custom 3D Gymnasium environment with simplified flight physics, 12-ray LiDAR sensing, procedurally generated terrain and moving observers with limited fields of view. I shaped a multi-objective reward and trained a PPO agent with Stable-Baselines3 and curriculum learning until it could reach its waypoints while staying out of the observers' cones.

I also built interactive visualisers in Pygame and Ursina, because watching a policy fly is a much faster way to learn what it has understood than reading a reward curve.
